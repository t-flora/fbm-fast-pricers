\begin{abstract}
We study Monte Carlo pricing of a fixed-strike arithmetic Asian call option under the
Rough Fractional Stochastic Volatility (RFSV) model~\citep{gatheral2018volatility},
in which log-volatility follows a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent
$H = 0.10$.  The non-Markovian structure of fBM forces every sample path to be drawn
from a dense $N \times N$ Gaussian distribution, requiring an $O(N^3)$ Cholesky setup
and an $O(MN^2)$ per-path simulation cost.  We implement and benchmark an exact dense
Cholesky baseline against two algorithms that exploit structure in the covariance
matrix: a circulant-embedding FFT method ($O(N \log N)$ per path, exploiting the
stationarity of fBM increments), and a global randomized SVD method ($O(Nk)$ per
path, exploiting low-rank structure).  We show that the circulant embedding is exact
for every $H \leq \nicefrac{1}{2}$, with smallest eigenvalue
$\Delta t^{2H}(N^{2H} - (N-1)^{2H})$ in closed form.  Over $N$ from 64 to 4000, the FFT
and rSVD costs grow linearly in $N$, while the Cholesky exponent rises from about 1 to
2.1 as the $N^2$ mat-vec takes over and its factor outgrows the cache.  The FFT method
overtakes Cholesky at $N \approx 250$ and is $7.6\times$ faster at $N = 4000$; drawing
two independent paths from each inverse transform was essential, since random number
generation, not the transform, dominates its per-path cost.  The rank-32 rSVD method is
$10\times$ faster than Cholesky at $N = 4000$ but underprices by $4$--$8\%$ at every
rank tested.  That bias follows the fraction of path variance the truncation discards
($13\%$ even at $k = 128$), which the usual Frobenius error ($1.2\%$) badly understates.
A variance-matched comparison shows that about 70\% of the rough-volatility premium on
the at-the-money Asian price is due to roughness itself.  Two extensions address the
remaining weaknesses: restoring the discarded variance as per-step noise removes the
low-rank bias, and a control variate built on the geometric Asian payoff, which has a
closed-form expectation given each volatility path, cuts the Monte Carlo variance by a
factor of 26--29 for every sampler.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Reproducibility}
All source code, benchmark scripts, and plotting code are available at
\url{https://github.com/t-flora/fbm-fast-pricers}.  Results can be reproduced
end-to-end by running \texttt{./run\_pipeline.sh} from the project root
(\texttt{--fast} gives a smoke test in a few minutes).  Each run writes a manifest
recording the git commit, parameters, and wall time to
\texttt{plots/runs/\textit{TIMESTAMP}/\allowbreak manifest.txt}.
